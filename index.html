Career Fair Strategy: a short checklist for the conversations themselves. The goal is not to memorize a script; it is to leave each worthwhile booth knowing what to do next.
Leave every good booth with these 3 answers
1. What specific job title or team should I search for?
2. When should a May 2027 graduate apply or watch for openings?
3. Who should I follow up with, or what is the best next step?
Immediately after the conversation
Capture just three things before moving on: the person’s name, one memorable detail from the conversation, and the promised next action. Those notes become the raw material for Lauren’s follow-up and, later, the Google Sheet tracker.
Universal backup questions
• Which entry-level roles are the best fit for a mechanical engineer with controls, testing and hands-on project experience?
• What separates the new graduates who do especially well on your engineering teams?
• How much hands-on design, testing, commissioning or manufacturing work do early-career engineers get?
• Are you hiring May 2027 graduates now, or when should I start watching your careers page?
• Is there a particular team, recruiter or job title you recommend I follow up on after the fair?
Don’t forget
Lead with Huffman + mechanical engineering, then adapt the rest of the story to the employer. Ask questions that cannot be answered by simply reading the company homepage. If the fit is weak, thank the recruiter and move on—Lauren’s time is more valuable at the strongest booths.
